How it works

  1. 1

    Pick your window

    Click into the document, editor or page you mean to work in.

  2. 2

    Press the shortcut

    ⌘⇧F by default, or any shortcut you set. Walls close in around that window.

  3. 3

    Hold to leave

    Holding a button for five seconds is the only way out. A pause long enough to ask: do I really want to stop?

Why it helps

Out of sight

Mail, chat and every other window disappear behind solid walls. What you can't see is easier to leave alone.

A deliberate exit

No quick click away. Five seconds is short when you mean it and long when you don't.

A journal on your Mac

Write before or after a session. It reads like a normal document and saves as Markdown files on your Mac.

Lives in the menu bar

Always one shortcut away, and out of your way the rest of the time.

What it is, and isn't

It is

  • A native Mac app for working in one window at a time
  • A local journal for notes on your sessions

It isn't

  • A website blocker
  • A security or parental-control tool

What Mac do I need?

macOS 14 or later.

Why does it ask for Accessibility access?

macOS asks for it so Deep Focus can work with the window you've focused and place the walls around it.

Can I change the shortcut?

Yes. ⌘⇧F is the default; you can set your own.

Where is my journal kept?

On your Mac, as Markdown files you can open in any editor.
